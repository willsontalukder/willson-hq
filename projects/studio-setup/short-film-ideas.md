# Short-film ideas: three portfolio pieces for Keep Rolling

Written by John (screenwriter) · 21 Oct 2026 · Task T04

Three short films (1–4 min) that Frank can write, shoot and cut alone in Dhaka in 1–2 shooting days each.
They are built around his real kit: Sony A7C, 24mm, 85mm, DJI Mic Mini 2 and Premiere Pro. There are
no lights, gimbal or tripod, and no colour grade, so the look has to come out of the camera finished.

**Why these three:** the Sep 15 night/rain reel had 3 likes but **14 shares**, the most of any post.
Moody pieces with a story get passed on. The Oct 14 post carried Facebook's "AI content" label and
did worst. So each piece is real footage with one clear feeling, and each one shows a client niche
what Frank can do for them.

| # | Title | Cast | Feeling | Niche it attracts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Keep Rolling** | 0 (city and objects) | Quiet awe that the city never stops | Brand / agencies, plus the page's signature piece |
| 2 | **Nanu's Bangles** | 1 friend | Being carried by the women who came before you | Wedding |
| 3 | **Before the First Cup** | 1–2 (barista and an optional customer) | The calm care behind something small | Café / restaurant, small brands |

## Rules that apply to all three (how the constraints become the style)

- **Frame rate and shutter:** shoot 4K **25p at 1/50**. Bangladesh mains power is 50 Hz, so this
  avoids flicker from shop tubes, LEDs and street lights. For slow motion use 1080p 100p at 1/100 or
  1/200, and check the screen for banding under artificial light before you rely on it.
- **Baked-in look, no grade:** don't use S-Log or HLG, because 8-bit log needs a grade Frank doesn't
  offer. Pick one picture profile or Creative Look per film and keep it the same for every shot.
  Set white balance by hand (Kelvin), not Auto: about 3800–4300K at night keeps the sodium and shop
  light warm, and about 5200–5600K works for daylight by a window. In Premiere, change only exposure
  and white balance with Lumetri basics.
- **No tripod:** use IBIS. Rest the camera on walls, railings, ledges, a tabletop or the camera bag,
  and brace your elbows for the 85mm. Locked-off shots resting on a surface are the stillness these
  films need. The 24mm is for handheld movement.
- **Sound:** no dialogue to record in sync. Each piece uses either a voice-over recorded later in a
  quiet room on the DJI Mic Mini 2, or nothing but ambience and music. Record 30–60 s of clean
  "wild" ambience at every location. Music must be **licensed only**, from a library where Frank
  holds a licence that covers social and portfolio use. Keep the licence record with the project.
- **Drone (DJI Neo 2):** not in any of these outlines. Most of Dhaka city is restricted airspace
  (airport approaches, cantonment, government areas) and needs permission from the civil aviation
  authority (CAAB), and night flying is a further problem. Add a drone shot only if `simon` confirms
  a specific spot is legal and permitted.
- **People in frame:** strangers stay wide or anonymous (hands, backs, silhouettes). Ask a nod
  before any close-up of a face. Anyone recognisable who is featured signs a simple release
  (friends included).
- **Light times in Dhaka, late Oct to Nov (approximate, `philip` to confirm on the day):** sunrise
  about 5:55–6:10 AM, sunset about 5:15–5:25 PM, blue hour about 20–25 minutes after sunset.

## 1. Keep Rolling (no actors, city and objects)

**Logline:** From the last light of the evening to the first tea of the morning, Dhaka never stops
turning. Wheels, fans, kettles and hands keep it rolling while the rest of the city sleeps.

**The one feeling:** quiet awe that the city never stops, and affection for the people who keep it
moving.

**Why it's good for the portfolio:** it carries the page's own name, so it works as the pinned
signature reel for "Keep Rolling". It extends the moody night format that already got shared most.
It needs nobody else, so it proves "one person, real footage, cinematic" straight after the
AI-labelled post. It also shows night exposure, composition and editing to rhythm with no lights,
which is exactly what agencies and brands want to see.
**Client niche:** brand and agency work (mood films, product launches, hospitality and city
campaigns). It is also a calling card for music-video work.

**Cast:** none. Strangers appear only wide or as hands and silhouettes.

**Locations (two zones, walkable):**
- Old Dhaka: Shankhari Bazar / Sadarghat side streets (rickshaws, workshops, tea stalls)
- A main road overpass or footbridge for the traffic shot (Karwan Bazar or any safe footbridge)
- A tea stall for dawn. Use one near home so the early start is easy.

**Visual motif:** everything that turns. Match-cut each circle to the next.

| # | Shot | Lens | Light / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook (0–5 s):** extreme close-up of a rickshaw wheel starting to spin, spokes catching the shop light. Hard cut to black, then the title "KEEP ROLLING". | 85mm | Night, shop-front light, about 7 PM |
| 2 | Wide street from a ledge, locked off: rickshaws, a bus and people stream through the frame, all blurred and warm | 24mm | Blue hour, about 5:35 PM |
| 3 | Ceiling fan turning above a workshop doorway, slow, seen from below (match-cut circle) | 24mm | Interior tube light, night |
| 4 | Hands on a sewing-machine wheel or a tailor's treadle. Rhythm. | 85mm | Night, practical bulb |
| 5 | From the footbridge, rested on the railing: headlight trails turning in a curve or a roundabout | 85mm | Night, about 8–9 PM |
| 6 | A rickshaw puller's bell hand and the painted rickshaw art, shallow focus, the street going soft behind | 85mm | Night, shop light |
| 7 | Empty street at 4–5 AM, one rickshaw crossing far away. The quietest frame of the film. | 24mm | Pre-dawn, about 5:00 AM |
| 8 | Kettle lid rattling, steam rising against the dark, the tea stall's single bulb | 85mm | Pre-dawn, about 5:30 AM |
| 9 | **Payoff:** tea poured from high into a glass in one long pour (100p slow motion), then a hand lifts it. Cut to the wide street waking up as the sun comes. End card: "Keep Rolling · Frank Willson Talukder". | 85mm, then 24mm | Sunrise, about 6:00 AM |

**Structure:** hook on the wheel (0–5 s), then the city in motion at night (middle, building in
pace), then stillness at 4 AM, then the first tea as the payoff. **Length:** 1:30–2:00.

**Sound and music:** no dialogue. Build the sound bed from real ambience: rickshaw bells, the
sewing machine, kettle rattle, the azan at dawn (handle it respectfully, keep it low and don't cut
it to the beat). Lay one licensed track that is pulse-driven and builds through the night, then
drops out at shot 7 so only the kettle and birds carry the dawn. For close sound like the kettle
and the bell, clip a DJI Mic Mini 2 transmitter close to the source.

**9:16 cut:** 30–40 s. Open on shot 1 (the wheel). Cut only the circles (wheel, fan, sewing wheel,
headlight curve, kettle) to the beat, one shot per bar, and end on the pour. Frame shots 1, 3, 4, 6
and 8 centred, so they crop cleanly from 4K. Use the on-screen text "Dhaka never stops." Post it as
the reel and pin it.

**Schedule (1 night into dawn, about 14 hours of work in total):**
- Day before: scout both zones in daylight for 1 hour and pick ledges and railings to rest on.
  Charge everything and format the card.
- 5:00–9:30 PM: shots 2 to 6 (blue hour first, then night).
- 9:30 PM–4:00 AM: go home, back up the cards, sleep.
- 4:30–6:30 AM: shots 7 to 9.
- Edit: 1 day for the main cut and 2–3 hours for the 9:16 cut.

**Risk and fallback:** some shopkeepers may say no to filming, so have two backup workshops in mind.
If it rains, that's a bonus: shoot reflections and keep the camera covered.

## 2. Nanu's Bangles (one actor)

**Logline:** On the morning of her wedding, a young woman gets ready alone with her late
grandmother's bangles. As each one goes on, she reads the letter she never got to send.

**The one feeling:** being carried by the women who came before you, with joy and missing someone
held in the same moment.

**Why it's good for the portfolio:** it is a getting-ready film without needing a real wedding. It
proves the skills wedding clients pay for: window-light portraits on the 85mm, details of the
jewellery, mehndi and sari, emotional pacing and a voice-over that makes families cry. The wedding
season in Bangladesh runs roughly Nov–Feb, so a film released by mid-November meets couples while
they are booking.
**Client niche:** wedding and holud (bride and groom getting-ready films, family-story edits).

**Cast:** 1 friend, female, comfortable on camera, who owns or can borrow a wedding or holud sari
and bangles. Her hands double for all the insert shots. The VO can be her voice or a different
friend's.

**Locations:**
- One bedroom with a large window (north- or east-facing is best for soft light), cleared of
  clutter. A mirror or dressing table is ideal.
- The same building's rooftop or verandah for the ending.

**Props:** glass or gold bangles, an old handwritten letter or envelope, one old photograph
(printed, not a real relative's if she prefers), a sari, mehndi if possible, marigolds.

| # | Shot | Lens | Light / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook (0–5 s):** close-up of bangles poured from an old cloth pouch into an open palm, a soft clink. VO, first line: "Nanu, you said I'd know when it was time." | 85mm | Soft window light, about 8:00 AM |
| 2 | Wide room from the doorway: she sits on the bed in window light with her back half to us. A still frame, the camera rested on the door frame. | 24mm | Window light, about 8:15 AM |
| 3 | The old photograph and the letter on the dressing table. Rack focus from photo to letter (handheld focus pull, or let her hand move into the plane of focus). | 85mm | Window light |
| 4 | Her hands with mehndi slide the first bangle on. Hold it. Slow motion at 100p. | 85mm | Window light |
| 5 | In the mirror: her face, half lit, eyes down, reading. She doesn't speak; the VO carries the letter. | 85mm | Side window light, about 8:30 AM |
| 6 | Handheld 24mm moving around her as she pleats and pins the sari. Quick and intimate, the busy middle. | 24mm | Window light |
| 7 | Detail sequence: the bangles stack up one by one, each new one clicking against the last (match cuts on each click) | 85mm | Window light |
| 8 | She folds the letter and tucks it into her blouse or anchal, close to her heart | 85mm | Window light |
| 9 | **Payoff:** rooftop at golden hour, wide. She lifts her wrist to the sun and the bangles catch the light. VO, last line: "I'm wearing you today." | 24mm, then 85mm close on the wrist | Golden hour, about 4:45–5:15 PM |
| 10 | End card over the bangles: "Nanu's Bangles · a film by Frank Willson Talukder". | — | — |

**Structure:** a sound-led hook (bangles plus the first VO line), a middle that rises as she gets
ready, and a payoff in golden-hour light. **Length:** 2:30–3:00.

**Sound and music:** dialogue-free on set. Write the VO as the letter, about 120–150 words in
Bangla with English subtitles, which widens the reach. Record it afterwards in a quiet room or
closet on the DJI Mic Mini 2, transmitter clipped about 15–20 cm from her mouth, several takes.
Clip a transmitter near her wrist for the bangle sounds. Music is one licensed track, soft piano
or strings or a gentle Bangla folk instrumental, that enters after the hook and swells only on the
rooftop.

**9:16 cut:** 45–60 s. Use the hook, shots 4, 5, 7 and 9, and keep the VO trimmed to the three
strongest lines. Shot 5 (the mirror) and 9 (the wrist in the sun) are the thumbnails. Caption:
"For every bride who wears someone's love on her wrist." Use burned-in Bangla and English
subtitles, because most people watch muted.

**Schedule (1 shoot day plus a VO session):**
- 2–3 days before: John finalises the letter VO, the actor approves it, and props are gathered.
- Shoot day: 7:30 AM set up and clear the room. 8:00–11:00 AM shots 1–8 (morning window light
  is softest). Break; the light is too hard at midday. 4:30–5:20 PM rooftop shots 9.
- Evening or next day: 1 hour of VO recording.
- Edit: 1–1.5 days including subtitles, plus 2 hours for the 9:16 cut.

**Risk and fallback:** if the room's window light is weak, shoot facing the window at an angle and
let her face carry the light. Don't push ISO into noise. If the rooftop is hazy, end at the window
instead with the bangles in a sunbeam at about 4:30 PM.

## 3. Before the First Cup (doubles as a brand piece)

**Logline:** Before the shutters go up, one barista opens a small Dhaka café alone, sweeping,
grinding and tasting, and the first customer of the day receives a cup that carries all of it.

**The one feeling:** the calm care hidden behind something small and everyday.

**Why it's good for the portfolio:** it is a short film and a ready-made café advert at the same
time. It shows a café owner exactly what Frank would make for them (product close-ups, the
craft, atmosphere in available light) and can be offered to the café to post, crediting Keep
Rolling. That puts Frank's work in front of their followers too.
**Client niche:** café and restaurant, and small product and food brands (bakeries, tea and coffee
brands, home businesses).

**Cast:** 1–2 people. The barista is best as the café's own staff (real hands, real skill) or a
friend who can make coffee. The optional first customer is a friend.

**Location:** one small independent café with a window, in Dhanmondi, Gulshan or Banani or
somewhere similar. Shoot before opening, about 6:30–9:30 AM. Approach the café is a job for
`andrew` and needs Frank's approval before any message goes out. The offer: free use of the film
in exchange for the location and early access.

| # | Shot | Lens | Light / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook (0–5 s):** black screen, then the shutter is rolled up and morning light floods in across the floor. The sound of the shutter does the hook. | 24mm, from inside, rested on the counter | Early daylight, about 6:45 AM |
| 2 | Barista ties an apron, flips on the light, the room wakes. Wide. | 24mm | Window plus practical light |
| 3 | Sweeping the floor. Low angle, dust in the window beam. | 24mm, handheld low | Side window light |
| 4 | Beans poured into the grinder, close. Then the grind. | 85mm | Window light |
| 5 | Tamping and pulling the shot: the crema forms (100p slow motion) | 85mm | Window light, about 7:30 AM |
| 6 | Barista tastes from a small spoon, a small nod. The face, in profile against the window. | 85mm | Backlit window |
| 7 | Steam wand and milk pouring. Latte art forms in one continuous take. | 85mm, overhead or rested at 45° | Window light |
| 8 | Wide: the café ready, chairs down, the "Open" sign turned. | 24mm | Morning light |
| 9 | **Payoff:** the first customer sits, the cup is set down, both hands around it, the first sip. Cut on the exhale. The café's logo card, then "Film by Keep Rolling". | 85mm, then 24mm wide | Morning light, about 8:30 AM |

**Structure:** shutter hook, a working middle in quiet rhythm, and the first sip as the payoff.
**Length:** 1:30–2:00 film. A 30 s ad version can be cut from the same footage.

**Sound and music:** no dialogue. The sound design is the star: shutter, broom, grinder, tamp,
espresso hiss, steam wand, cup on saucer. Clip a DJI Mic Mini 2 transmitter near the machine and
another near the counter, and record each sound clean as a wild take after the shot. Use one
licensed, warm, minimal track (acoustic guitar or soft lo-fi) at low level so the café sounds sit
on top. Check that the licence covers commercial use if the café posts it.

**9:16 cut:** 20–30 s, cut hard to the sounds: shutter, grind, tamp, crema, pour, sip, one sound
per shot. Hook: the shutter rising in the first second. Frame shots 4, 5, 7 and 9 centred for the
crop. Text: "Every first cup starts before you arrive." Give the café a clean copy without the
Keep Rolling end card for their own feed, with Frank credited in their caption.

**Schedule (1 morning shoot plus a short pickup slot):**
- About 1 week before: find the café and get a yes (`andrew`; Frank approves the message). Scout
  for 30 minutes at the same morning time to see the window light.
- Shoot day: 6:15 AM arrive and set up. 6:45–9:30 AM shots 1–9 in story order. 9:30–10:00 AM
  wild sound and spare product inserts (pastries, menu, logo) for the café.
- Edit: 1 day for the film, the 9:16 cut and the café's 30 s ad version.

**Risk and fallback:** if the café can only give 1 hour, drop shots 3 and 8 and get them from
outside. If the barista is shy, the face appears only in shot 6 and everything else is hands.
