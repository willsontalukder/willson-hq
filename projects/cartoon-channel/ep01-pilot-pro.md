## 1 · How a TV cartoon is built, and what we copy

**Cutting rhythm.** A TV cartoon almost never holds one shot for 8 seconds. It cuts every 1.5–4 s, and each shot shows one idea: a look, an action, a reaction. Veo gives us 8 s clips; we use the best 1.5–3.5 s of each. Many short shots = energy. Few long shots = the "static" feel Frank saw in his test.

**Coverage pattern.** Every scene is built the same way:
1. **Establishing (WS):** where are we, who is here, where are they standing.
2. **Medium (MS):** the character and what they are doing.
3. **Close-up (CU / ECU):** the important detail (the scoop, the face).
4. **Reaction:** the other character's face. Comedy lives in reactions; the joke lands on the face, not on the object.
Then back out to a wide when the geography changes (someone runs, jumps, lands).

**180° rule and screen direction.** Draw an imaginary line between Moto and Mishti. Every camera stays on the same side of that line (the near side of the lane, see the map). Result: Moto is always on the left looking right, Mishti always on the right looking left, and the audience never gets lost. Movement keeps its direction too: Mishti's leap goes right-to-left (towards Moto) every time we see it.

**Match-on-action cuts.** Cut in the middle of a movement, and start the next shot in the same pose at a new size. Moto lifts the cone in the wide, we cut mid-lift to the medium. The eye follows the movement and never notices the cut. This is also how we hide Veo's weak spots: the hard moment (the scoop leaving, the catch, the landing) happens across a cut.

**The animation principles that matter here**
| Principle | What it means | Where we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | A small move the opposite way before the big move (crouch before a jump, lean back before a lick) | Moto's wind-up lick (S02), Mishti's crouch (S07) |
| **Squash & stretch** | Soft things flatten on impact and stretch when moving fast | Scoop squashes under the tongue then stretches as it pops (S03); Mishti stretches in the leap (S08); Moto squashes when she lands (S09) |
| **Overshoot & settle** | A move goes a bit past its end pose, then springs back and settles | Moto stopping on his mark (S01), Mishti landing (S09), scoop landing on the cone (S10) |
| **Holds** | A frozen beat so the audience can read a face | The Oops Face freeze (S04), the scoop hanging at the top of its flight (S05) |
| **Arcs** | Natural movement travels on curves, not straight lines | The scoop's flight, Mishti's leap, Moto's spin and plop |
| **Timing** | Fast action, slow reaction (snap, then hold) | Pop is 4 frames, the stare that follows is 12+ frames |
| **Follow-through** | Loose parts keep moving after the body stops | Scarf ends, Moto's floppy ear, tail, the gamcha ends |

**Motivated camera moves.** The camera moves only when something gives it a reason: it cranes down to arrive in the lane, pushes in slowly when a character is thinking, tilts up because the scoop flies up, tracks because Moto runs, bumps when Mishti lands. A push-in on a reaction is the cheapest, most "TV" move there is. A camera that moves for no reason looks like AI; a camera that never moves looks like Frank's first test.

## 5 · Keyframes and Flow prompts, shot by shot

**How the chain works.** For continuous action across a cut, the **end keyframe of shot N and the start keyframe of shot N+1 show the same pose**, re-framed to the new shot size. Make the end keyframe of N first, then attach it as an extra reference when you make the start keyframe of N+1 and ask for "the same pose, closer". That is what keeps the pose, the cone paw and the scoop count identical across the cut.

**Image references for every keyframe:** `Mishti_sheet_v1.png`, `Moto_sheet_v1.png`, `loc-lane.png` (+ the previous keyframe where noted). If a keyframe drifts off-model, paste the full LOCK text for that character from `character-bible.md` into the prompt.

**Video:** Frames to Video, 9:16, start frame always; end frame where one is given. The fixed tail on every video prompt is: *smooth professional 3D TV-cartoon animation, 24 fps cartoon timing, squash and stretch, no speech, no music, no on-screen text, no people.*

## 6 · Workflow

### In Flow
1. **Set up the project.** New Flow project, aspect ratio **9:16**. Upload `Mishti_sheet_v1.png`, `Moto_sheet_v1.png`, `loc-lane.png`.
2. **Make all keyframes first** (image generation, references attached). Order: S01 start → S01 end → S02 start (attach S01 end) → ... down the chain. 2–4 takes each; keep the best, name it `EP01_S01_start.png`, `EP01_S01_end.png` and so on. Images are cheaper than video, so fix problems here.
3. **Build a still animatic before any video.** Drop the start keyframes into a Premiere sequence (1080×1920, 24 fps) at the edit durations from the shot list, with the scratch SFX. Watch it on a phone. If the story reads with stills, it will read in motion. If a cut confuses you, fix the keyframe, not the video.
4. **Animate.** Frames to Video: start frame, end frame where given, paste the video prompt. Do 1 test with the **Fast** model first; when the move and action are right, run 1–2 takes on the **Quality** model. If your model choice doesn't allow an end frame, run start-frame only and cut before the end.
5. **If a clip comes out static,** put the move at the very front of the prompt in plain words ("The camera moves: slow push-in.") and try again. If it still won't move, generate it static and add the push-in in Premiere (below). Don't burn more than 3 Quality takes on any shot (the production plan's limit): simplify the action instead.
6. **Upscale** the chosen take to 1080p in Flow before downloading (punch-ins need the pixels). Name files `EP01_S03_t2.mp4`.
7. **Log** generations per shot on the dashboard task, so we learn the real cost of this method.

### Per-clip QC (before a clip goes into the timeline)
- [ ] **On-model:** Moto's up-ear, floppy ear, gamcha checks and knot; Mishti's green eyes, red-and-white scarf, tail tip. Sizes right (Mishti is head-sized next to Moto).
- [ ] **Screen direction:** Moto faces screen-right, Mishti faces screen-left, stall on the right, rickshaw on the left.
- [ ] **Not mirrored:** with Moto facing right, his up-ear is the near ear.
- [ ] **Prop in the right paw:** cone in Moto's right paw, paws never open. Cup in both of Mishti's paws once she has it.
- [ ] **Scoop count:** matches the continuity sheet for that shot (2, then 1, then 2).
- [ ] **Camera move present** and the right kind (push, track, tilt, crane).
- [ ] **No floating objects**, no extra cones, cups or scoops appearing.
- [ ] **Usable section:** the 1.5–3.5 s you need is clean, even if the rest of the 8 s isn't.
- [ ] No people, no letters on signs or rickshaws, nobody hurt.

### Fixing it in Premiere
- **Speed ramps (Time Remapping).** Veo is usually slower and floatier than cartoon timing. Speed the action part to 130–200% and leave the hold at 100%. A fast-in, slow-out ramp on the pop (S03) and the leap (S08) makes them snap.
- **Holds.** S04: Frame Hold Segment of 6 frames on the Oops Face. Also use a 4–8 frame hold at the top of the scoop's flight (S05) if Veo doesn't hang it.
- **Punch-ins.** Keyframe Scale 100 → 115% over 2 frames on S04 for the comedy hit. A static clip can get a fake slow push-in: Scale 100 → 108% over the whole shot, ease in/out.
- **Hard cuts only.** No crossfades, no dissolves, no transitions. TV cartoons cut. Cut on the action frames listed in the shot list.
- **Flip (Horizontal Flip effect) only when the whole take is a true mirror.** Use this table:

| What you see | What it means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Moto faces right, up-ear is the near ear | Correct | Keep |
| Moto faces left, up-ear is the near ear | True mirror | **Flip** it (tight shots only; in a wide the stall and rickshaw swap sides and give it away) |
| Moto faces left, up-ear is the far ear | Correct dog, wrong direction | Don't flip (it breaks the ear). Regenerate |
| Moto faces right, up-ear is the far ear | Wrong ear | Don't flip. Regenerate or recut around it |

- **Floating prop at the end of a take:** cut before it happens. That's why every prompt puts the action early.
- **Sound:** Veo audio is a guide track only. Rebuild every hit from a licensed SFX library (YouTube Audio Library first). Half a beat of silence before the scoop lands in the cup (S08) and on the loop frame (S11). Light bouncy music bed, no vocals, ducked under the hits.
- **Export:** 1080×1920, H.264, 24 fps. Watch it on a phone, loop it three times, then send it to Thomas for QC.

## 7 · Honest limits

| What Veo still can't do reliably | Workaround in this plan |
|---|---|
| **Following a named camera move every time.** It often gives a static shot or drifts. | Move goes first in the prompt; start/end keyframes at different framings force the move; slow push-ins can be faked in Premiere with Scale keyframes; whip pans are done in the edit (a 3–4 frame directional blur cut), not asked of Veo. |
| **Precise physics** (a scoop landing exactly in a cup, an object leaving a paw). | The hard moments sit across cuts (pop exits frame in S03; catch in S08 is short and covered by the landing in S09). End keyframes pin where things finish. |
| **Props staying in paws.** Paws open and the object floats (Frank's test). | Prompts never ask paws to open; "cone stays in his paws" is written in. Cut before any float. |
| **Left/right consistency** (Moto's ears, which paw). Veo mirrors shots, and the character sheet's side view faces left, which pulls takes that way. | Keyframes set the direction; the mirror table above says when a flip fixes it. If it keeps fighting, make one extra reference image of Moto in profile facing right and attach it. |
| **Real holds.** Veo keeps everything moving. | Holds made in Premiere with Frame Hold. |
| **Cartoon timing.** Veo's motion is smooth but slow and even. | Speed ramps; the beats in each prompt are written fast so the 8 s clip has spare handles. |
| **Two characters touching** (Mishti landing on Moto's head, the cheek lick): scale and contact drift. | Keyframes pin both poses; keep the contact moment short; S09 and S10 have the most takes budgeted. |
| **Timings inside a prompt** ("0–1 s") are guidance, not law. | Treat them as order of events. The edit sets the real timing. |
| **Stray letters on signs/rickshaws, extra characters.** | QC check; regenerate or crop with a punch-in. |
