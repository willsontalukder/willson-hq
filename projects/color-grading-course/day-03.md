# Day 3 · White balance (29 Oct)

## Lesson (10 min)
1. **What a colour cast looks like on the Parade:** find something that should be neutral (white wall, grey floor, white shirt, black suit). On the **RGB Parade**, the three channels should sit at the **same height** in that area. Blue higher than red = cool cast. Red higher than blue = warm/orange cast. Green higher = the "tube light" green you get indoors in Dhaka.
2. **The two sliders** (Lumetri → Basic Correction → White Balance):
   - **Temperature:** blue ↔ orange. Fixes daylight vs tungsten.
   - **Tint:** green ↔ magenta. Fixes tube lights, LED panels and leaf-green bounce.
   - Order: Temperature first, Tint second.
3. **The eyedropper** (WB Selector) is a quick start, not the answer. Click it on something truly neutral and **not clipped** (not a blown-out window). Then check the Parade and fine-tune by hand.
4. **Correct vs keep the mood:** a church with warm candle or tungsten light *should* feel a little warm. Neutralise the white, then push Temperature back up a few points so the room still feels like the room. Skin is the final judge (tomorrow's lesson).
5. **Your footage:** on Friday you used a custom white balance, so most clips should only need small fixes. Mixed light (window + tube light) is where you'll see the biggest cast.

## Task (30–40 min)
1. Same 3 clips again (church, outdoors, restaurant). Duplicate the Day 2 sequence.
2. On each clip, find one neutral area. Hover over it and look at the **Parade**: write down which channel is high.
3. Fix it with **Temperature first, then Tint**, by hand, until the three channels line up in that area. Then try the eyedropper and compare: which was closer?
4. On the church clip, after neutralising, add **+5 to +10 Temperature** to bring back the warmth. Decide by eye which version feels right.
5. Find the shot with the worst green/magenta problem (tube light or LED) and fix it with **Tint** only.
6. Export a before/after still for each clip (Shift+E).
