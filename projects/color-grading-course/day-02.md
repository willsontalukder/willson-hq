# Day 2 · Exposure with the waveform (28 Oct)

## Lesson (10 min)
1. **Three zones on the waveform (IRE):**
   - **Blacks / shadows:** 0–20. The darkest real black (hair, a black suit) should touch about **0–5**, not float at 15 (looks milky) and not pile up flat at 0 (detail lost).
   - **Midtones:** 20–70. Skin lives here: **about 55–70** for lighter skin in good light, **40–60** for darker skin or a moodier scene.
   - **Highlights:** 70–100. A white shirt or a sky can sit at **85–95**. Anything flat at 100 is clipped and can't be brought back.
2. **Which slider moves what** (Lumetri → Basic Correction → Light):
   - **Exposure** moves the whole trace up or down. Set it first, for the face.
   - **Contrast** stretches the trace from the middle outwards.
   - **Highlights / Shadows** move the upper / lower part gently (they protect the extremes).
   - **Whites / Blacks** set the very top and very bottom points. Use them last, to place the brightest white and the deepest black.
3. **Your footage:** Friday was shot with Picture Profile off, Creative Style Standard, 8-bit. It's already contrasty, so small moves (±0.3 exposure, ±15 on the others) go a long way. Big pushes on 8-bit cause banding in skies and walls.
4. **Order every time:** Exposure → Whites/Blacks → Highlights/Shadows → Contrast. Check the face last.

## Task (30–40 min)
1. Use the same 3 clips as Day 1 (church, outdoors, restaurant). Duplicate the Day 1 sequence so you keep Day 1's version.
2. On each clip, reset Basic Correction and redo the light in the order above, watching **only the waveform** (cover the image with your hand for the first pass if you have to).
3. Hover over a face in the Program monitor and find its height on the waveform. Write down the IRE number for each clip.
4. Find one shot where the highlights clip (window, sky, church lights). Bring **Highlights** down until the window shows detail, then check that the face didn't get dark. If it did, raise Exposure a little.
5. Export one before/after still per clip (Shift+E).
